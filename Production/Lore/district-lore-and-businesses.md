# Neko-Tokyo: twelve districts and Mars

Working series bible · 7 November 2026. User-confirmed facts are separated from proposed expansion. **Production priority: Season 1, Episode 1, 24 scenes, exactly 20:00.** New district characters stay written concepts until the pilot is finished. No new voices or district artwork are commissioned by this document.

## Confirmed world rules

- There are exactly **12 districts**, not 13.
- District 1 is ultra-rich; 2 rich; 3 upper class; 4 upper middle class; 5 and 6 middle class.
- District 7 is where the cats reside and the five Kitty Punks operate.
- Districts 8–9 are forced-labor camps for cats. Districts 10–11 are the pits.
- District 12 is lawless “kitty hell”: dogs and cats are sent there and **can never return**. Do not undermine this with routine escape missions or a convenient secret tunnel.
- Humans live on Mars. **Feeelon Musk**, half Elon Musk and half cat, rules all the people there. This is a fictional satirical character. His exact origin, appearance, powers and relationship to Barkington remain undeveloped.
- The hero crew remains Trixie / CatGirl, Byte, Ghost, Onyx and Neon. District allies never silently become a sixth permanent hero.
- The Daily Bone is the dog establishment paper, in red and black. The Catnip Times is the cat resistance paper, in purple and black. Both sides sometimes read or mock the other's coverage. The political-party analogy is about competing editorial camps, not literal real-world party affiliation.
- Barkington's Dalmatian-spotted **Dog Royce** is his personal driving-around-town car. The separate black restaurant limousine remains available for his entourage or formal arrivals.

## Proposed organizing idea

The lower a district's number, the less its residents must see the cost of their comfort. Food, energy, manufactured goods and labor move upward; waste, debt, punishment and unwanted citizens move downward. Goodboy Global profits from the separation. This mechanism is proposed lore, not a claim that every rich resident is villainous or every poor resident heroic.

District 7 remains the emotional home of the series: the warehouse, living room, hacker lair, shopkeepers, workers and games give the revolution something personal to protect. Existing factory material places the salmon complex in Sector 7; do not relocate it to the pits just because it is industrial.

## Businesses

### District 1: user-named luxury brands

**Chewy Vuitton** — luxury trunks, handbags and travel goods; **Pawda** — fashion and accessories; **Permes** — heritage leather goods and elite personal appointments. Watch brands: **Catier**, **Pawlex**, **Bonelot**. Preserve these exact spellings. Their inspirations are Louis Vuitton, Prada, Hermès, Cartier, Rolex and Hublot respectively; design their fictional identities consistently rather than changing the names between shots.

Proposed additions: **The Gilded Bowl**, reservation-only dining; **Pedigree Private**, a discreet concierge service. Establish a recurring shopping street before generating six unrelated storefront sets.

### Districts 2–3: recreation and social life

User-named: **Doggieland**, the dog amusement park; **Alley Cats**, bowling; **Main and Tale**, barbershop. District 2 has the exclusive flagship experiences; District 3 has accessible branches and local clubs.

Six proposed businesses:

1. **Fetch & Furious** — electric kart racing; employee **Dex Lap** knows every track shortcut.
2. **Pawradise Club** — pools, tennis and expensive membership; manager **Pearl Paddle** polices the guest list.
3. **The Velvet Leash** — supper club and live music; singer **Vera Velvet** hears deals made between sets. Distinct from the existing dog named Velvet.
4. **Nine Lives Arcade** — competitive gaming; owner **Nix Highscore** respects skill more than pedigree and could become a Kitty Punks contact.
5. **Purrfect Frames** — cinema; projectionist **Reel** sometimes screens films the distributors would rather forget.
6. **Bone Appétit** — polished neighborhood bistro; chef **Basil Broth** notices premium deliveries arriving while lower districts go hungry.

### Districts 4–7: proposed everyday businesses

- **D4:** **Nest & Rest** home goods, **Bright Paw Academy** tutoring, **The Trim Hedge** garden café. Possible recurring proprietor: **Fern**, a gardener whose clients underestimate the staff.
- **D5:** **Spare Paw Auto**, **Whisker Basket** groceries, **Button & Bone** repairs and alterations. These serve households, not luxury tourists.
- **D6:** **Last Stop Noodles**, **Tailwind Couriers**, **The Daily Grindstone** commuter café. Their opening hours follow the transport shifts.
- **D7:** Retain existing **7-Catleven**, **Nip Fuel**, **Pawzza** and The Catnip Times. Proposed additions: **Second Chance Circuits**, a repair and reclaimed-parts shop, and **The Loose Cable**, a tiny community music venue. Don't displace established locations to add these.

### Districts 8–11: user-named food chains and proposed local trade

**Kidsdonalds** and **Ketucky Fried Human** operate in the forced-labor camps and pits. Keep those exact spellings. Proposed presentation: aggressively happy advertising above cheap food counters and miserable working conditions. What is literally in the meals is **not established**; a brand name alone does not settle that lore.

Proposed additional businesses: **ShiftMart** (camp essentials on account), **Quota Cup** (canteen kiosk), **Rust & Found** (D10 salvage exchange), **Deepwell Exchange** (D11 repair and barter). Recurring counter workers should be residents with motives, not merely background jokes.

### District 12: proposed survivor-run places

**Last Bowl** — communal kitchen; **No Questions Repair** — tools and clothing; **Ashlight Market** — barter ground; **The Final Stop** — shelter and information exchange. These names describe fragile local institutions, not ordinary corporate franchises. No travel agency, return ticket, or business that invalidates permanent exile.

## Season introduction plan — proposed, not locked episode scripts

- **Season 1: District 7 first.** Finish the 20-minute pilot. The factory and shortage reveal the supply hierarchy. Upper-district branding and camp destinations can be clues without full tours or new casts.
- **Season 2: Districts 4–6, then 8–9.** The crew discovers why middle-class residents tolerate the system, then encounters the labor sustaining it. Camp residents drive their own resistance; the five heroes cannot free everyone with one hack.
- **Season 3: Districts 1–3, then 10–11.** Contrast curated pleasure with the pits. Follow money and erased records upward and downward. The existing proposed human-return finale can be retained, but the delegation must come from Mars under Feeelon's rule.
- **Season 4 and beyond: Mars and District 12.** Reveal Feeelon's society and the cost of permanent exile. District 12 can first appear through a resident viewpoint or an arrival that does not return. Do not send the main crew there for a reversible day trip. The exact order remains flexible.

Feeelon should expand the story rather than secretly excuse Barkington. Barkington and Goodboy knowingly committed their own exploitation. Humans moving to Mars is current user canon; the older draft's environmental-emergency explanation is an unconfirmed, superseded proposal. No District 13, no generic affluent D2/D8 canal concept overriding the class hierarchy.
